\chapter{Discussion}
\label{chap:discussion}

\section{Overview}

This dissertation combined the replication of five published medical-data studies with a standardised \gls{xai} extension. The findings did not reveal one explanation pattern that applied across every experiment. They instead showed that explanations were shaped by the design of the predictive study, the fitted model, the representation of the features, the explanation method, and the variability introduced by repeated fitting.

The discussion is organised around the five research questions introduced in Chapter~\ref{chap:introduction}. The key findings are interpreted first for the individual datasets and then for the framework as a whole. Particular attention is given to the distinction between computational reproducibility and clinical validation. The experiments provide evidence about model behaviour and explanation consistency, whereas clinical validation would require external data, prospective evaluation, and assessment with intended healthcare users.

\section{RQ1 - Reproducibility of the Published Workflows}

RQ1 examined the extent to which the five published workflows could be reconstructed and the methodological ambiguities that affected this process. All five studies were reproducible at the procedural level permitted by the available data and methodological descriptions. Their main preprocessing stages and model families were implemented, and each experiment produced a final analytical configuration for the \gls{xai} analysis. Exact numerical reproduction was less consistently achievable.

The Heart experiment illustrates the importance of implementation detail. Logistic-regression and \gls{svm} configurations were comparatively well specified, whereas labels for neural-network architectures did not uniquely determine their initialisation, optimisation, internal structure, or stopping behaviour. The replicated neural-network results could therefore differ even when the broad model type matched the source study \citep{khanna_comparative_2015}. A further implementation correction converted the \gls{grnn} inputs from pandas objects to numeric NumPy arrays. This ensured that the algorithm processed observation rows rather than DataFrame column labels. The correction demonstrates how an apparently minor data-structure assumption can invalidate an implementation even when its conceptual description is appropriate.

The Stroke experiment exposed the relationship between severe class imbalance and the choice of evaluation measure. High accuracy could coexist with weak positive-class performance. The selected class-balanced logistic-regression model achieved high recall but low precision, making this trade-off visible rather than concealing it behind majority-class accuracy. Restricting feature selection and learned preprocessing to the training data also provided a methodologically stronger analytical configuration than one in which the complete dataset informed correlation screening \citep{wu_machine_2024}.

The Diabetes replication showed that the preferred configuration depended on the evaluation objective. The eight-feature \gls{rf} produced the highest \gls{rocauc} among the selected configurations, while the five-feature \gls{rf} produced the highest accuracy, recall, and F1 score. No feature subset was therefore uniformly superior. The five-feature model was retained because it was specified through the analytical design and supported comparison across all three global explanation methods. It was not selected retrospectively from the test results \citep{chang_pima_2023}.

The \gls{ckd} experiment provided the clearest demonstration of preprocessing leakage. Fitting imputation and encoding before the train--test split changed the observed performance, particularly for the \gls{dt} and gradient-boosting models. The numerical effect was not identical across classifiers. However, its direction does not determine whether leakage occurred. Once the complete dataset contributes to learned preprocessing parameters, the test partition is no longer fully independent. Retaining the source-style branch while using a leakage-controlled branch for \gls{xai} preserved the distinction between reproducing the published procedure and correcting its evaluation design \citep{santhiya_chronic_2024}.

\gls{hbaone} required a different interpretation because the source study used an observational, full-cohort inferential analysis rather than a held-out predictive benchmark \citep{strack_impact_2014}. The study was reconstructed as a logistic-regression analysis with grouped covariates and interactions. Supplying this fitted model to the shared \gls{xai} interface broadened the test of framework compatibility, but it did not convert the analysis into an externally evaluated classifier. The absence of held-out evaluation, rather than the observed in-sample metrics, is the reason its predictions cannot be interpreted as evidence of out-of-sample generalisation \citep{collins_tripodai_2024, moons_probastai_2025}.

Together, the replications demonstrate that reproducibility has several levels. Access to the datasets and executable workflows enabled procedural reconstruction. Exact numerical equivalence remained dependent on reporting detail, random procedures, library behaviour, and implementation choices. Explicit assumptions, leakage-controlled analytical branches, saved inputs, runtime metadata, and automated tests made these limitations visible rather than silently replacing missing methodological information.

The answer to RQ1 is consequently qualified. All five workflows were procedurally reconstructable, while incomplete reporting, implementation choices, and differences in analytical design limited exact numerical reproduction.

\section{RQ2 -- Agreement Between Global Explanation Methods}

RQ2 examined the consistency of native importance, permutation importance, and \gls{shap} rankings. Agreement varied considerably across the fitted models and feature representations. This variation is an important result because it identifies the conditions under which explanation methods provide mutually supporting evidence and the conditions under which their different definitions lead to different accounts of model behaviour.

For Heart, Stroke, and \gls{ckd}, native importance was more strongly correlated with \gls{shap} than with permutation importance. Native importance reflects estimator-specific parameters or structures, while \gls{shap} distributes differences from an expected prediction across the model inputs. Permutation importance instead measures the reduction in a selected performance score after the information carried by a feature has been disrupted. Predictor dependence, substitutable information, and the selected scoring measure can therefore cause permutation rankings to differ from native and \gls{shap} rankings \citep{fisher_models_2019, strobl_conditional_2008, NIPS2017_8a20a862, vilone_classification_2021}.

The Diabetes model produced identical feature rankings under all three methods. This agreement is informative, particularly because glucose occupied the leading position in every ranking. It should nevertheless be interpreted in relation to the model's five-feature design. All predictors necessarily belonged to every top-five set, meaning that complete set overlap was guaranteed. The identical ordering supplied the stronger evidence of agreement.

\gls{hbaone} differed from the four predictive configurations. Ranking its design terms by absolute coefficient magnitude produced almost no agreement with \gls{shap}, whereas \gls{shap} and permutation importance produced similar rankings. The largest coefficients belonged to interactions involving the youngest age group and medical-specialty categories. A sparse interaction can have a large conditional coefficient while applying to relatively few observations. Aggregated \gls{shap} and permutation importance instead summarise attribution magnitude or predictive reliance across the analysed cohort. The observed difference therefore reflects the quantities measured by the methods rather than establishing that one method was erroneous \citep{fisher_models_2019, NIPS2017_8a20a862, linardatos_explainable_2020, vilone_classification_2021}.

These findings support the use of multiple global methods as complementary diagnostic views. Strong agreement indicates that the methods prioritise similar features. Weak agreement identifies a need to examine feature dependence, encoding, sparsity, interactions, scoring choices, and method assumptions. Explanation quality cannot be reduced to one universal measure \citep{mohseni_multidisciplinary_2021, lofstrom_meta_2022}.

RQ2 shows that global explanation methods were complementary rather than interchangeable. Their level of consistency depended on the fitted model, the feature representation, and the definition of importance being applied.

\section{RQ3 - Local Explanations for Representative Cases}

RQ3 examined how \gls{lime} explanations differed among correct, incorrect, and borderline predictions and how they related to global importance. The revised analysis used up to ten observations per available category and 20 perturbation seeds per observation. It comprised 212 selected observations overall; \gls{ckd} contributed only one false positive and one false negative because no further errors of those types were present. The resulting local rules were specific to individual predictions but were not necessarily specific to correct or erroneous outcomes.

In Heart, chest-pain type led every representative explanation, although different value intervals supported different predicted classes. In Stroke, high age strongly supported both the true-positive and false-positive predictions, while low age supported both the true-negative and false-negative predictions. In Diabetes, high glucose led both a true positive and a false positive. In \gls{ckd}, serum creatinine led both a correct positive prediction and a false positive.

A local explanation describes which rules contributed to the model's output around a selected observation. It does not explain the true clinical outcome or certify that the prediction is correct. A coherent explanation of a false positive therefore remains an explanation of erroneous model behaviour. Presenting such an explanation without the observed outcome and uncertainty context could encourage inappropriate reliance \citep{ribeiro_why_2016, vilone_classification_2021, vasconcelos_explanations_2023, lee_understanding_2023}.

The Stroke cases were particularly informative because the fitted model produced confident errors. The false-positive probability was 0.986, while the false-negative probability was 0.024. Neither prediction was close to the decision threshold. The local analysis therefore complemented the performance metrics by identifying the rules that supported incorrect high-confidence predictions. This illustrates why confidence and interpretability should not be treated as evidence of correctness \citep{vasconcelos_explanations_2023, lee_understanding_2023}.

The prominence of the \texttt{bmi\_missing} indicator provided a second Stroke audit finding. It appeared among the five leading features under all three global methods and led the borderline local explanation. Because the indicator records whether \gls{bmi} was observed rather than a physiological measurement, its influence shows that the model used information from the data-recording process. This signal may reflect patterns of measurement, care, or subgroup composition and should be investigated before the model is transferred to another setting. It should not be interpreted as a biological effect of missing \gls{bmi}.

Global-local feature presence averaged 0.996 for Stroke, 1.000 for Diabetes, 0.763 for \gls{ckd}, 0.724 for Heart, and 0.373 for \gls{hbaone}. This lexical overlap did not constitute formal agreement between global \gls{shap} and local \gls{lime}. A feature name could occur in both analyses with a different direction, interval, scale, or contextual meaning because \gls{shap} and \gls{lime} use different explanation procedures and reference structures \citep{NIPS2017_8a20a862, ribeiro_why_2016, vilone_classification_2021}. Complete overlap in Diabetes was partly structural because its model contained only five features. \gls{hbaone} continued to demonstrate the interpretive difficulty of sparse, high-dimensional design terms.

Perturbation-seed sensitivity was limited for the leading rules. Mean pairwise rank correlations ranged from 0.898 to 0.969 and mean top-three overlap ranged from 0.892 to 0.998. This strengthens confidence that the reported leading rules were not artefacts of one perturbation sequence. It does not establish robustness to alternative background data, kernel settings, discretisation procedures, or case-selection strategies.

Mean absolute \gls{lime} weights were slightly larger for correct cases than for error cases in each applicable dataset. The cases were not random samples, however, and \gls{ckd} provided only two errors. The observed differences therefore support no population-level inference. Representative-case analysis is better understood as a transparent audit procedure for examining selected successes, errors, and boundary predictions \citep{ribeiro_why_2016, mohseni_multidisciplinary_2021}.

RQ3 provides a stronger but still descriptive answer. Repeating \gls{lime} across observations and perturbation seeds demonstrated that the leading local rules were generally stable, while exposing case-specific model behaviour and the rules associated with prediction errors. Explanation content alone still did not distinguish correct predictions from errors or demonstrate formal agreement with global importance.

\section{RQ4 - Stability Under Bootstrap Model Refitting}

RQ4 examined the persistence of global permutation-importance rankings when the composition of the final training matrix changed. For each retrainable configuration, 100 stratified bootstrap samples were drawn with replacement, the model was refitted, and permutation importance was evaluated on the fixed held-out test set using negative log loss. Fixing the model and permutation seeds focused the comparison on variation induced by the bootstrap training samples. This design differs from the previous seed-only procedure, which could not induce model variation for deterministic logistic regression.

The bootstrap-refit analysis produced moderate-to-strong complete-ranking stability across all four datasets. Mean Spearman correlations were 0.700 for Heart, 0.812 for Stroke, 0.849 for Diabetes, and 0.949 for \gls{ckd}. Mean top-three overlap followed a different ordering: 0.645 for Heart, 0.741 for Stroke, 0.890 for Diabetes, and 0.759 for \gls{ckd}. Complete-ranking correlation and leading-feature overlap therefore captured related but non-identical aspects of persistence.

Heart showed the lowest stability, although \emph{ca} and \emph{thal} remained its most persistent features. In Stroke, age appeared in the top three in every bootstrap refit, while the encoded marital-status indicators also appeared frequently. Glucose and \gls{bmi} were the most persistent Diabetes features. In \gls{ckd}, specific gravity appeared in the top three in every refit and packed cell volume appeared in 82\% of refits.

The \gls{ckd} result changed materially after replacing accuracy with negative log loss. Accuracy on 120 test observations changes in increments of $1/120$ and had produced many tied zero permutation importances, making the ordering of weak features sensitive to arbitrary tie resolution. Negative log loss used predicted probabilities and produced more graduated importance values. Under this common scorer, \gls{ckd} had the highest mean complete-ranking correlation rather than the lowest. This demonstrates that a stability estimate depends on the scoring rule and experimental perturbation as well as on the fitted model \citep{fisher_models_2019, lofstrom_meta_2022, canha_functionally-grounded_2025}.

Predictive performance, within-fit agreement among explanation methods, and bootstrap-refit stability remain distinct properties, even though \gls{ckd} scored highly on all three in the present analysis. Heart had strong discrimination but the lowest bootstrap-refit stability, while Diabetes had lower predictive performance but strong cross-method agreement and leading-feature persistence. The results therefore provide no basis for inferring explanation stability directly from predictive performance.

No \gls{hbaone} estimate was included in the principal stability comparison because its saved bundle did not contain a validated retraining callback capable of producing equivalent bootstrap refits. Substituting a simpler model or different workflow would have changed the object being evaluated.

A separate grouped-permutation sensitivity experiment reconstructed a Statsmodels refitting callback and confirmed that the projected runtime was feasible. However, only 58 of 100 bootstrap refits converged. Treating the 58 successful fits as representative could bias the stability estimate if convergence depended on the resampled cohort. The exploratory result was therefore retained as evidence of feasibility but excluded from the principal cross-dataset stability comparison.

RQ4 was answered within this bootstrap-refit design. Rankings showed dataset-dependent persistence under changes in training-sample composition, with different orderings for complete-ranking correlation and top-three overlap. The experiment did not assess alternative test partitions, preprocessing uncertainty before construction of the aligned matrices, \gls{shap} background samples, or repeated \gls{lime} perturbations; each would address a different form of explanation stability.

\section{RQ5 - Framework Robustness and Generalisability}

RQ5 examined which framework components remained applicable across the represented datasets and model families. At the software-execution level, the framework operated successfully across all five configurations. It supported scikit-learn linear classifiers and \glspl{rf}, the tested statsmodels result wrapper, DataFrame inputs, and a Patsy interaction matrix. Native importance, permutation importance, \gls{shap}, representative-case selection, and \gls{lime} were generated for every final model. Stability analysis was generated whenever the corresponding notebook provided a retraining function.

The reproducibility infrastructure strengthened this evidence. Dataset-level exports and fitted bundles reduced dependence on the active notebook state. Environment locks, runtime metadata, configuration files, and timestamped command-line runs documented how the outputs were produced. Comparison reports, 62 passing automated tests, and 82\% statement coverage verified the implemented operations within the tested scope.

Measured bundle reruns also identified distinct computational bottlenecks. Total execution ranged from 31.6 seconds for Stroke to 221.4 seconds for Diabetes among the four held-out predictive configurations, with repeated-seed \gls{lime} accounting for most of their execution time. \gls{hbaone} required 2,211.5 seconds (36.9 minutes), of which 2,122.9 seconds were spent in the global stage. This reflected the use of model-agnostic \texttt{PermutationExplainer} over a 69,970-row, 91-column design matrix. Technical compatibility therefore did not imply uniform computational efficiency; cost depended on matrix size, explainer class, model prediction cost, local-case count, and seed count. The single-run measurements are descriptive and hardware-dependent.

The comparison in Table~\ref{tab:xai-framework-comparison} further clarifies the scope of this contribution. Unlike broad multi-modal or presentation-oriented libraries, the framework was designed as a focused tabular research-audit workflow. Its distinctive combination is not a new attribution algorithm, but the orchestration of complementary global and local methods with outcome-based case sampling, agreement measures, repeated-seed \gls{lime} analysis, bootstrap-refit stability, and reproducible cross-dataset artefacts. Conversely, it does not provide the modality breadth of OmniXAI, the interactive facilities of InterpretML, DALEX, or Shapash, or the catalogue of attribution-quality metrics available in Quantus.

Scientific generalisability was narrower. The model-family summaries contained only two linear configurations, two tree ensembles, and one statsmodels configuration. Dataset, model family, feature count, sample size, class prevalence, preprocessing, and clinical task varied together. The higher mean agreement observed for the tree ensembles therefore cannot be attributed to tree models generally. It was influenced by perfect agreement in the five-feature Diabetes configuration and by the high bootstrap-refit correlation observed for \gls{ckd}. Broader claims would require multiple datasets and models within each family under a design that separated these factors \citep{mohseni_multidisciplinary_2021, vilone_classification_2021}.

\gls{hbaone} tested an important boundary of the framework. The software accommodated a 91-column interaction design matrix containing 90 non-intercept terms. However, its explanations referred to encoded design terms that were more difficult to translate into raw clinical concepts. The model had no held-out evaluation or retraining callback, and its model-agnostic \gls{shap} computation was considerably more expensive. Software compatibility therefore did not remove the methodological consequences of the underlying study design \citep{strack_impact_2014, NIPS2017_8a20a862, vilone_classification_2021}.

The available evidence supports bounded generalisability. The framework is reusable across the tested binary-classification interfaces and produces a consistent set of outputs across heterogeneous configurations. Support for multiclass classification, regression, survival analysis, other statistical-model specifications, or unstructured clinical data would require corresponding implementations, tests, and empirical evaluations. Claims concerning clinical deployment would additionally require external and prospective validation, calibration assessment, evaluation with healthcare professionals, and analysis of clinical utility \citep{collins_tripodai_2024, moons_probastai_2025, vasey_decideai_2022}.

RQ5 was answered conditionally. The framework was operationally reusable across the five tested configurations, while its scientific generalisability remained dependent on the task, dataset, representation, and availability of a reproducible training procedure.

\section{Methodological Implications and Clinical Boundaries}

The findings indicate that validation of the predictive workflow should precede explanation analysis. Explanations cannot correct information leakage, inappropriate model selection, or an evaluation design that does not support the intended claim. Once the predictive configuration has been established, comparing complementary importance methods can reveal whether the same features remain influential under different definitions. This comparison is particularly relevant when correlated predictors, sparse interactions, or metric-specific reliance can alter feature rankings \citep{fisher_models_2019, strobl_conditional_2008, vilone_classification_2021}.

Explanation stability also requires an explicit operational definition. Stability under repeated model fitting, changes in the explanation background, perturbations of individual observations, and alternative data samples concern different sources of variability. Reporting the evaluated source of variation makes the result interpretable and prevents stability from being treated as an inherent property of an explanation method \citep{lofstrom_meta_2022, canha_functionally-grounded_2025}.

Local explanations should be presented with the observed outcome, predicted class, probability, and case-selection rationale. The confident Stroke errors demonstrate that coherent local rules can accompany incorrect predictions. Encoded interactions require additional care because a valid design-column explanation may not correspond to an intuitively meaningful or actionable clinical concept \citep{ribeiro_why_2016, vasconcelos_explanations_2023, lee_understanding_2023}.

The study does not demonstrate that the explanations are clinically useful or safe. Clinical plausibility was not assessed by healthcare professionals, and no explanation interface was evaluated in a clinical workflow. Agreement and stability are computational properties rather than substitutes for external validation, calibration, subgroup analysis, prospective evaluation, or human-factors research \citep{collins_tripodai_2024, moons_probastai_2025, van_calster_calibration_2019, vasey_decideai_2022}. Clinical \gls{xai} development also requires participation by intended users and evaluation of how explanations affect reliance and decision quality \citep{panigutti_co-design_2023, allgaier_how_2023}.

\section{Strengths of the Study}

The main strength of this work is its integration of replication and explanation. The study did not begin with undocumented fitted models. It reconstructed the preprocessing, modelling, and evaluation context from which each explanation was generated. Separating paper-style and corrected analytical branches also made methodological departures visible without erasing the procedures reported by the source studies.

A further strength is the use of complementary explanation perspectives. Native importance, permutation importance, \gls{shap}, and \gls{lime} were retained as distinct outputs rather than collapsed into a single score. Rank agreement, top-feature overlap, representative-case analysis, and bootstrap-refit stability revealed both convergence and disagreement among these views.

The heterogeneity of the five experiments also provided a demanding test of the framework. The datasets differed in outcome, size, feature representation, class distribution, model family, and analytical purpose. This variation limits controlled cross-dataset comparison, but it provides meaningful evidence about which software interfaces and analytical outputs transferred across configurations.

Finally, the software contribution was supported by controls extending beyond interactive notebook execution. Configuration files, dependency locks, provenance metadata, saved bundles, timestamped reruns, result comparisons, demonstrations, and automated tests made the implemented workflow more inspectable and reproducible.

\section{Limitations}

The study has several limitations. The datasets are retrospective and originate from public or previously prepared research sources. Their representativeness, measurement processes, missingness mechanisms, and temporal relevance differ. No external validation cohort was used.

Only one final analytical configuration was explained per dataset. The results therefore characterise selected fitted models rather than the complete set of plausible models. Model selection was pre-specified to avoid test-set optimisation, but alternative defensible models could produce different explanations.

The framework's predictive scope is binary classification. The \gls{hbaone} configuration is not directly comparable with the four held-out prediction tasks because it uses in-sample inference on the full cohort. Its inclusion broadens software testing but also increases analytical heterogeneity.

Permutation importance can be affected by correlated features and unrealistic permutations. Aggregated \gls{shap} results depend on the explainer, reference distribution, output scale, and feature-dependence assumptions. \gls{lime} depends on local perturbation and discretisation choices. None of the methods provides causal attribution \citep{NIPS2017_8a20a862, ribeiro_why_2016, vilone_classification_2021}.

The principal stability experiment used 100 bootstrap refits of each final aligned training matrix and one operational definition based on permutation rankings evaluated with negative log loss on a fixed test set. It did not characterise uncertainty from alternative data partitions, preprocessing pipelines, \gls{shap} backgrounds, or repeated \gls{lime} perturbations. \gls{hbaone} was excluded from that comparison because its exploratory callback converged for only 58 of 100 resamples.

Representative cases were deliberately selected and are not a population sample. Although the revised \gls{lime} analysis used up to ten observations per category, this purposive selection prevents inferential comparison of correct and erroneous predictions; \gls{ckd} also contained only one error of each type. The global-local overlap measure captured lexical feature presence only and did not compare signs, magnitudes, or local surrogate fidelity.

No clinician evaluation, usability study, fairness audit, causal analysis, prospective trial, clinical-utility assessment, or regulatory conformity assessment was performed. Conclusions are therefore limited to computational model behaviour, explanation consistency, bootstrap-refit stability, and framework execution.

\section{Discussion Summary}

The combined evidence supports a cautious view of \gls{xai}. Explanation methods were useful for exposing recurring feature reliance, disagreement among importance definitions, confident prediction errors, and instability under retraining. They did not produce a single authoritative account of clinical reasoning.

Procedural replication was achievable across the five studies, although exact numerical reproducibility was constrained by reporting and implementation details. Cross-method agreement was high in some fitted models and low in others. Stability differed substantially even among models with strong predictive performance. Local explanations clarified model behaviour around selected observations but did not distinguish correct from incorrect reasoning automatically. The framework generalised operationally across the tested interfaces while remaining scientifically bounded by task, data, and representation.

These findings establish the basis for the final conclusions. They support reproducible, multi-method explanation analysis as a model-audit practice while rejecting the stronger claim that computational explanations alone validate a model for clinical use.

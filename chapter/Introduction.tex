\section{Context and Motivation}

The increasing availability of electronic health records, public clinical datasets, and computational resources has expanded the use of \gls{ai} and \gls{ml} in medical prediction. These methods have been applied to diagnosis, prognosis, risk stratification, and hospital-outcome prediction, often using heterogeneous combinations of demographic, physiological, laboratory, and administrative variables \citep{hassija_interpreting_2024, bharati_review_2024, ahmed_artificial_2022}. Their ability to model complex and potentially non-linear associations makes them attractive for \glspl{cdss}, particularly when conventional statistical summaries do not capture all relationships present in the data \citep{ahmed_artificial_2022, bharati_review_2024, tjoa_survey_2021}.

Predictive performance alone, however, is insufficient to establish that a model is reliable, clinically meaningful, or suitable for deployment. Reported results can be influenced by data cleaning, missing-value treatment, feature encoding, class imbalance, train-test partitioning, feature selection, model-selection procedures, and \gls{dataleakage}. These decisions are not always described with enough precision to permit exact reproduction. Consequently, an apparently strong result may be difficult to reproduce or may depend on methodological choices that limit its validity beyond the original experiment \citep{collins_tripodai_2024, moons_probastai_2025,van_calster_calibration_2019}.

The interpretation of fitted models introduces a second layer of uncertainty. Many predictive models operate as \glspl{blackbox}, providing limited direct insight into the variables driving their outputs. \Gls{xai} methods attempt to address this limitation by producing \glspl{globalexplanation} or \glspl{localexplanation} for individual predictions \citep{adadi_peeking_2018, barredo_arrieta_explainable_2020, linardatos_explainable_2020}. Nevertheless, different explanation methods quantify different objects. Model coefficients or impurity-based importance, permutation importance, \gls{shap} values, and \gls{lime} weights are not interchangeable, and agreement among them cannot be assumed \citep{NIPS2017_8a20a862, ribeiro_why_2016, vilone_classification_2021}.

These two concerns - the reproducibility of predictive experiments and the consistency of their explanations - motivate this dissertation. \gls{xai} outputs should not be treated automatically as valid accounts of clinical reasoning, because they describe aspects of model behaviour and do not independently establish causality, clinical validity, or safe use \citep{tjoa_survey_2021, yang_unbox_2021, vilone_classification_2021}. Accordingly, this study examines how several established explanation methods behave when applied through a
common framework to models obtained from five heterogeneous medical-data replications.

\section{Problem Statement}

Medical \gls{ml} studies frequently differ in their datasets, outcomes, preprocessing procedures, model families, evaluation metrics, and reporting quality. This heterogeneity complicates both reproduction of published findings and comparison of explanation results. Even when the same explanation methods are used, differences in feature representation, model structure, sample size, and class distribution can produce substantially different rankings and local rationales.

Three connected problems are therefore addressed. First, published predictive workflows must be reconstructed carefully enough to distinguish the reported procedure from assumptions introduced during replication. Second, model selection and final evaluation must avoid using the held-out test set as a tuning instrument. Third, explanations must be analysed according to what each method measures rather than being treated as equivalent evidence of clinical relevance.

The research problem is consequently formulated as follows: \emph{how can a reproducible \gls{xai} framework be applied across heterogeneous replicated medical-prediction studies, and what can the resulting global, local, agreement, and stability analyses legitimately reveal about model behaviour?}

The study is computational and retrospective. It evaluates models and explanation artefacts derived from existing datasets. It does not assess clinical effectiveness, establish causal relationships, validate explanations with healthcare professionals, or propose the
resulting models for deployment in clinical practice.

\section{Aim and Objectives}

The general aim of this dissertation is to develop and evaluate a reproducible framework for applying and comparing global and local \gls{xai} methods across replicated medical-prediction studies.

The specific objectives are:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item Reproduce five published analyses concerning heart disease, stroke, diabetes, \gls{ckd}, and hospital readmission associated with \gls{hbaone} measurement, while documenting ambiguities and methodological limitations in the reported workflows;
    \item Distinguish paper-style replication from the corrected analytical configuration used for the \gls{xai} extension whenever exact imitation would introduce leakage, inappropriate evaluation, or another avoidable methodological limitation;
    \item Apply a common \gls{xai} pipeline comprising native model importance where supported, permutation importance, \gls{shap}, and \gls{lime} explanations for representative cases;
    \item Assess agreement among global feature-ranking methods within each dataset and model;
    \item Evaluate bootstrap-refit feature stability where the original training procedure can be rerun through a retraining callback;
    \item Compare global and local explanation behaviour across datasets without treating raw importance magnitudes or differently encoded variables as directly equivalent;
    \item Assess the reproducibility, robustness, and generalisability of the implemented framework within its tested binary-classification scope.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Research Questions}

The dissertation addresses the following research questions:

\begin{description}
    \item[RQ1] To what extent can the predictive workflows reported in five medical-data studies be reproduced, and which methodological ambiguities or limitations affect their replication?

    \item[RQ2] How consistently do native importance, permutation importance, and \gls{shap} rank influential features within each final dataset-specific model?

    \item[RQ3] How do local \gls{lime} explanations differ across representative correct, incorrect, and borderline predictions, and how do they relate to global importance patterns?

    \item[RQ4] How stable are global feature rankings across repeated model fits when the original training workflow can be rerun?

    \item[RQ5] Which components of the standardised \gls{xai} framework remain applicable across heterogeneous datasets and model families, and what limits its generalisability?
\end{description}

The research questions are primarily descriptive and comparative. They examine the behaviour, agreement, and stability of explanation methods rather than testing predefined clinical hypotheses. Agreement between explanation methods is not interpreted as evidence of clinical
safety.

\section{Research Design Overview}

The study follows a replication-and-extension design. Five published studies were selected through a structured literature search and study-selection process. Their datasets and analytical procedures were reconstructed in dataset-specific replication notebooks. Where the original description was ambiguous, the implementation recorded the assumption adopted. Where a paper-style procedure conflicted with leakage-controlled
evaluation \citep{moons_probastai_2025}, the replication result was retained for comparison, while a corrected branch was used to define the final model for explanation.

Each selected model and its aligned analysis data were then passed to a shared \gls{xai} framework. For the four predictive configurations, these data included the corresponding training and test partitions, whereas \gls{hbaone} retained its full-cohort analytical matrix. The framework
generated predictive metrics, native importance when available, permutation importance, global \gls{shap} summaries, rank-agreement outputs, representative cases, local \gls{lime} explanations, and bootstrap-refit stability results when retraining was available. Dataset-level outputs were subsequently analysed in a separate cross-dataset notebook.

The use of a common framework standardises the execution and export process but does not eliminate differences among the underlying studies. In particular, the \gls{hbaone} experiment uses a large observational cohort and an interaction-rich logistic-regression design matrix containing expanded categorical terms and interactions. It therefore serves as a deliberately heterogeneous test of framework applicability rather than as a directly comparable disease-prediction task.

Reproducibility was supported through fixed random seeds where applicable, dataset-oriented result directories, saved model bundles, dependency locks, runtime-version metadata, automated tests, bundle validation, timestamped command-line reruns, and tolerance-aware comparison of canonical and regenerated outputs.


\section{Contributions}

The principal contributions of this dissertation are:

\begin{itemize}
    \item A documented replication of five heterogeneous published medical-data studies, including explicit separation between paper-style reproduction and methodological correction where required;
    \item A reusable binary-classification \gls{xai} framework that applies a consistent set of global and local explanation procedures to scikit-learn estimators and the tested statsmodels model configuration;
    \item An analysis of agreement and disagreement among native importance, permutation importance, and \gls{shap} feature rankings;
    \item A representative-case procedure covering true-positive, true-negative, false-positive, false-negative, and decision-boundary cases when those categories are available;
    \item A bootstrap-refit assessment of feature-ranking stability for experiments that expose their training procedure to the framework;
    \item A cross-dataset synthesis that identifies which framework capabilities transfer across model families and which conclusions remain dataset- or representation-dependent;
    \item A reproducible software and verification workflow comprising automated tests, configuration-based execution, provenance metadata, independent reruns, and result-comparison reports.
\end{itemize}

These contributions provide a reproducible methodology and software framework for auditing predictive models, comparing complementary \gls{xai} methods, examining representative prediction errors, and evaluating the stability of global feature rankings. They support more transparent and critical assessment of medical machine-learning models before any clinical evaluation is attempted.


\section{Scope and Limitations}

The implemented framework is limited to binary classification and the model interfaces evaluated in the five experiments. The study used retrospective datasets obtained from public research repositories, including datasets that had previously been curated for research use \citep{khanna_comparative_2015, wu_machine_2024, chang_pima_2023, santhiya_chronic_2024, strack_impact_2014}. Differences in their sampling processes, missingness mechanisms, outcome definitions, and feature representations limit direct comparison and external generalisability \citep{moons_probastai_2025}.

Explanation outputs are method-dependent. Native importance, permutation importance, \gls{shap}, and \gls{lime} have different definitions and scales, so their raw magnitudes are not compared directly \citep{fisher_models_2019, NIPS2017_8a20a862, ribeiro_why_2016, vilone_classification_2021}. Rank correlation and top-feature overlap describe similarity in ordering or feature presence, but they do not demonstrate that an explanation is clinically correct. Bootstrap-refit stability indicates whether selected global features persist when the training sample is perturbed, but it does not establish robustness to every possible source of data, model, or explanation variability \citep{lofstrom_meta_2022, canha_functionally-grounded_2025}.

The representative-case analysis provides transparent examples of model behaviour across correct, incorrect, and borderline predictions rather than population-level estimates. Together with the global agreement and stability analyses, these cases provide a computational basis for identifying model behaviour that may warrant further investigation. Future work can extend this foundation through clinician-centred assessment, prospective evaluation, subgroup and fairness analysis, causal investigation, clinical-utility studies, and regulatory assessment \citep{moons_probastai_2025, vasey_decideai_2022}. The conclusions of this dissertation are therefore interpreted within its computational model-audit scope.

\section{Dissertation Structure}

The remainder of this dissertation is organised as follows. Chapter~\ref{chap:state_of_art} reviews \gls{xai} in medical prediction, explanation methods, reproducibility concerns, and approaches for evaluating agreement and stability. Chapter~\ref{chap:methodology} describes the study-selection process, the five replication workflows, the final analytical configurations, the standardised \gls{xai} framework, and the cross-dataset analysis. Chapter~\ref{chap:replication_results} documents the reconstruction of the five source workflows and the corresponding replication findings. Chapter~\ref{chap:validation_results} evaluates the final analytical configurations and reports predictive uncertainty for the four held-out models. Chapter~\ref{chap:xai_results} presents global explanations, agreement between methods, bootstrap-refit stability, representative local cases, and cross-dataset comparisons. Chapter~\ref{chap:discussion} interprets the findings in relation to the research questions and existing literature while distinguishing computational evidence from clinical claims. Chapter~\ref{chap:conclusions} summarises the contributions, limitations, conclusions, and priorities for future work. Appendix~\ref{app:local_explanations} provides supplementary representative \gls{lime} explanations.
